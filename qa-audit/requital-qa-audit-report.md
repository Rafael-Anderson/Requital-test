# Requital QA Audit

*Full-platform QA pass across `requital-backend` (NestJS, :3000), `requital-admin` (Next.js, :3001), and `requital-storefront` (Next.js, :3002), conducted 2026-08-20 against the local dev environment (seeded shop `test-shop`, admin login `admin@test-shop.com`). Every finding below was verified by reading the actual component/route source and driving the live apps with Puppeteer at 1440×900 and 390×844, unless explicitly marked otherwise. Screenshots referenced by filename live in `qa-audit/screenshots/` alongside this report.*

---

## Dashboard

The dashboard (`admin/app/dashboard/page.tsx`) loads and renders correctly against real seeded data: Avg Basket Value, Total Orders, Total Revenue, the Sale Overview line chart, Outlet Distribution, Sales Activity funnel, Customer Growth, Sales Distribution by Channel, and Top Selling Products all populate correctly from a single seeded order. "Experience Rating" is hardcoded to 0.0 / "No reviews yet," which the source comment at `page.tsx:83-91` explains plainly: there is no review/rating model in the schema anywhere in the platform. That's a deliberate, self-documented gap rather than a bug, but it's worth naming here since it recurs as a real feature absence on the storefront PDP as well (see Storefront, below).

The most significant finding on this page is that a feature CLAUDE.md documents as shipped does not actually exist. The documentation states that `shop.productEditorMode` was "extended beyond the product form to gate UI on Orders, Customers, and the Dashboard too," and specifically that simple-mode shops get `components/SimpleDashboard.tsx` (a pared-down Revenue Today / Orders Today / Top 3 Products view) in place of the full week/month dashboard. Neither claim holds up: `admin/components/SimpleDashboard.tsx` does not exist anywhere in the repository, there is no reference to `SimpleDashboard` in any `.tsx` file in `admin/`, and `admin/app/dashboard/page.tsx` has no `useShopMode()` call or `isSimple` branch of any kind. This is directly testable against the seeded shop, whose `productEditorMode` is `"simple"` — confirmed via `GET /shop`. So a florist who has opted into the pared-down, simple-mode Orders and Customers experience elsewhere in this exact app is nonetheless shown the full eight-widget analytics dashboard with no way to get the simpler view. This reads as either a regression (the file was deleted after the documentation was written) or documentation written ahead of an implementation that was never finished, and it's the single highest-value dashboard finding to act on.

A second, purely visual defect: `DonutChart.tsx` uses a monochrome grayscale ramp (`stroke-black`, `stroke-zinc-400`, `stroke-zinc-300`, `stroke-zinc-200`) for its segment colors rather than distinct hues. With the single seeded data point at 100% in one segment, both "Outlet Distribution" and "Sales Distribution by Channel" render as a solid black ring with no color-coding visible at all. Even with more segments, three of the four ramp steps are pairwise low-contrast grays that would be genuinely hard to distinguish at a glance — categorical data needs distinguishable hues, not shades of the same color. This gets worse on mobile: the donut legend labels truncate to single characters at 390px width ("Main Branch" renders as "M," "draft_order" as "d.") because the flex allocation between the fixed-size SVG ring and the legend text column leaves no room for the label once the viewport narrows.

Related to that same widget, both it and the order-detail views elsewhere in the app render the raw backend `channel` column value verbatim — `"draft_order"` appears unhumanized in the legend, rather than something like "Draft order." `backend/src/dashboard/dashboard.service.ts:188` passes `channel: (g.channel as string | null) ?? 'Unspecified'` straight through with no label mapping, and the field is documented backend-side as having "no real attribution system behind it yet" — a known-shallow field, but the frontend doesn't even apply the trivial underscore-to-space humanization every other snake_case value in this app gets (order statuses, for instance).

Compared to Shopify: there's no click-through from any stat card to a filtered order list, no "compare to previous period" beyond a small delta-percentage badge, no scheduled or emailed reports, and no export. Compared to a UAE-market competitor like Dukany: there's no WhatsApp order-volume metric despite the backend having a dedicated platform-owned WhatsApp order-alert pipeline already built (`common/whatsapp.ts`), and outlet breakdowns have no per-Emirate view.

## Orders

### Live Orders Kanban

Core functionality was verified end to end: opening the board, clicking into an order, advancing its status via "Accept," and watching it move from New Orders into In Preparation with a correct toast — all work exactly as documented, including the compare-and-swap status transition and the 20-second polling refresh.

The most substantial structural finding here is that **there are three separate, differently-featured order-detail UIs in this app, and which one a merchant sees depends entirely on which link they clicked, not on any documented rule.** `OrderDetailModal.tsx` (used on the History page, and on the kanban in advanced mode) is the fullest: Details/Invoice tabs, a status timeline, internal notes, returns, an editable delivery fee, and an external-delivery combobox. `SimpleOrderDetailModal.tsx` (kanban only, simple mode) is a deliberately trimmed subset. But a *third*, structurally distinct full-page view at `admin/app/orders/[id]/page.tsx` is reached from three separate entry points — the Customer Detail page's order rows, the Cmd+K command palette, and the "Converted to Order #X" link after completing a draft order — and it has none of the modal's polish: status changes go through a bare native `<select>` rather than the labeled action buttons used everywhere else, there's no status timeline, no invoice tab, and critically **no simple/advanced-mode branching at all** — a simple-mode shop still sees the full internal-notes/returns/external-delivery sections here that the kanban deliberately hides from them.

That same standalone page also still renders through `PageShell variant="form"` — exactly the narrow variant CLAUDE.md's own history documents as deprecated for the Product Wizard, for the same reason: at 1440px the content column ends around x≈940px, leaving roughly 500px of empty gray background to the right. Its loading-state skeleton is also a raw `<div className="max-w-3xl space-y-4">` sitting entirely outside `PageShell`, a direct violation of the documented "every page routes its top-level content through PageShell" rule, even if only during the transient loading state.

A smaller but real cross-cutting inconsistency: every "Cancel order" action in this app — the kanban card, both detail modals, and the standalone page — funnels through a bare browser `window.confirm()` dialog rather than the app's own animated `Modal` component. It's applied consistently, so it isn't a functional bug, but it sits oddly against a design system this deliberate about custom toasts and a documented "modals close only via X/Cancel, never backdrop click" rule — the native, unstyleable browser confirm is the one place that whole system gets bypassed.

For a florist SaaS specifically, one gap stands out: **neither the kanban card nor `SimpleOrderDetailModal` shows the delivery date or time slot anywhere when the shop is in simple mode.** The kanban card explicitly gates the delivery-date field behind `!isSimple`, and `SimpleOrderDetailModal`'s entire body (customer name/phone, "Placed X ago," items, total) has no delivery-date field at all. For a same-day-delivery flower and gift business, this means a simple-mode merchant literally cannot see when an order needs to go out anywhere in the Orders UI without switching the whole shop out of simple mode — a significant miss for the exact vertical this product targets, and one of the more consequential findings in this audit.

On mobile, the kanban degrades to a horizontally-scrolling four-column strip with only about 1.3 columns visible at 390px and just a sliver of the next column as the only "there's more" signal — workable, but with no scroll affordance (arrow, fade, dot indicator) making it discoverable.

Compared to Shopify: no order tags, no saved or custom views, no bulk actions from the kanban itself (only History has bulk status-update and CSV export), and no fulfillment tracking number field anywhere — the External Delivery feature captures carrier, vehicle, price, destination, and status, but never a tracking number or URL. Compared to Dukany and UAE norms more broadly: despite the backend having full platform WhatsApp order-alert integration, **the customer phone number is rendered as plain text, never a `tel:`/`wa.me` link, in every one of the three order-detail views** — a small, cheap fix that would meaningfully help a UAE merchant who wants to message a customer directly from an order.

### Order History

Functionally solid: debounced search across reference/customer/phone, pagination, bulk status update plus CSV export, and a correctly-formatted total all work, and this page correctly always opens the full `OrderDetailModal` regardless of shop mode (which CLAUDE.md documents as intentional — History stays a full-featured, if unlinked, route for simple-mode shops).

One clear, citable convention violation: the row action here is a full `Button` reading "View," not an icon-only button. CLAUDE.md's own "Row actions on list/table pages are icon-only buttons" section is explicit that this exact treatment — a full button with visible text — previously existed on other pages before being deliberately converged away from, and explicitly says not to reintroduce it. This page reintroduces it. The same raw `"draft_order"` channel value from the Dashboard also appears here, both in the table and inside the modal's Order Info panel.

On mobile, the table shows only Ref No, Status, and Customer Name within the 390px viewport; Type, Payment Mode, Grand Total, Channel, Placed At, and the View action are all pushed off-screen with no visible scroll affordance at all — no shadow, fade, or scrollbar hint that more columns exist. The table is technically horizontally scrollable, but nothing signals it, and Grand Total — arguably the single most important field in an order-history row — is invisible by default on a phone. Missing versus Shopify: no date-range filter specific to this page (the Dashboard has one, History doesn't), export is selection-only rather than "export all matching search," no saved filters, no order tags.

### Draft Orders

Verified fully functional end to end: filled customer and address fields, selected a product, added a line item, submitted, opened the resulting draft, and clicked "Mark as paid," which correctly converted it into a real order. The two-column form/summary layout at 1440px is well composed with no dead-space problem.

Structural issues: `admin/app/orders/draft-orders/new/page.tsx` is a bare `<div>` with no `PageShell` at all — a direct violation of the page-width convention, even though the hand-built grid happens to render acceptably in this case. On the list page, only the customer's name text is a clickable link — the rest of the row gives no hover affordance and isn't clickable, which makes this a third, different row-interaction pattern within the same Orders section: History uses an explicit "View" button, Customers makes the whole row clickable, and Draft Orders makes only a substring of one cell clickable. There is also no search, filter, or pagination on this list, and no bulk actions at all, unlike History and Customers which both have a selection-driven bulk-action bar.

Compared to Shopify draft orders: no partial payments, no scheduled reminder emails, no duplicate-draft action — this is a single linear open → invoice-sent/completed/cancelled flow with just "Send invoice" and "Mark as paid."

### Abandoned Carts

Could only be verified against its empty state in this pass, since the storefront checkout path needed to naturally populate this list was blocked by the seeded shop's unpublished status. The empty state itself is clean, correctly worded, and correctly points the merchant at Business Information for the recovery-email toggle.

One structural observation independent of data: this table has zero row actions at all — not even a View — where every other list page in this audit has at least one action affordance per row. There's also no way from the admin UI to preview what a populated row or a recovery email actually looks like without waiting for a genuine abandoned checkout, which is a real onboarding blind spot for a merchant trying to verify the feature works before trusting it. Compared to Shopify's "Abandoned checkouts" tab, which offers a manual "send recovery email now" action per row, this table is read-only and driven purely by the shop-wide toggle, with no per-row manual trigger.

## Customers

**The single most severe, concrete bug found in the Orders/Customers/Dashboard slice of this audit is here.** The Customer Detail page's order-history sub-table renders the raw, untrimmed, full-precision `DECIMAL(65,30)` value for Grand Total instead of a formatted currency figure — a real seeded order's total renders as `149.00000000000000000000000000000000 AED` instead of `149.00 AED`. This is fully root-caused, not just observed: `CustomersService.findOne` runs a plain `SELECT *` against the `order` table and spreads the raw row directly into the response with zero `trimDecimal()` calls anywhere in the file — confirmed by grep that `customers.service.ts` never even imports the helper. Contrast this with `OrdersService.findOne`, which explicitly trims `total`, `deliveryFee`, `taxAmount`, `discountAmount`, `giftCardAmount`, `priceAtPurchase`, the payment amount, and the external-delivery price before ever returning a response — exactly the pattern CLAUDE.md documents `trimDecimal()` as existing to enforce at every API response boundary. This is a one-line fix (mirroring the same call its sibling service already makes), but as shipped it makes the product look badly broken to any merchant checking a repeat customer's order history, and it will reproduce on any customer with at least one order.

On a more positive note, Customers is the one page in this audit where the documented simple/advanced-mode gating is actually implemented correctly, in direct contrast to the Dashboard gap above: the list correctly swaps in an Email column and drops the bulk-select/`BulkActionBar` in simple mode, and the detail page correctly hides the four stat cards and the Birthday field. Search, sortable columns, and inline contact-info editing all work as expected.

Clicking an order row on Customer Detail navigates to the standalone `/orders/[id]` full page rather than opening the same modal used elsewhere in the app — another instance of the three-order-UI inconsistency documented under the Kanban section above. On mobile, the same column-visibility problem recurs: Lifetime Value, Last Order, and Email are all pushed off-screen at 390px with no scroll affordance.

Missing versus Shopify: no customer tags, no customer notes or timeline, no segments or saved filters, no merge-duplicates tool, and — notably — no "Add customer" button anywhere on the list page; a customer record can only ever be created as a side effect of placing or drafting an order, so a merchant can't pre-populate a known customer's phone book entry ahead of their first order. Compared to Dukany and UAE-market expectations: the same missing click-to-WhatsApp gap as in Orders — the phone number is prominent everywhere on this page but is never a live `tel:`/`wa.me` link, despite the backend's platform WhatsApp integration.

## Products

### Product List

The product list is considerably more feature-complete than its section in this audit's brief implied. Row selection drives a full bulk-action bar: bulk status change, a bulk price-adjustment modal, CSV export in the exact column order the CSV importer expects for round-tripping, and a confirm-gated bulk delete. A "New product" split-button also offers CSV import, which does a stateless preview-then-confirm (the same file is uploaded twice; nothing is written until the merchant explicitly confirms). This is a solid, Shopify-parity feature set for list management with no gap to report.

Per-row actions correctly follow the "icon-only button, one per column" convention: edit, adjust stock, transfer stock, duplicate, and delete each render as a tooltip-wrapped icon button. Status is a clickable pill rather than a `Toggle` component, which is arguably an exception to the documented "Toggle, not Checkbox" rule, but it predates that rule and is defensible since the pill also carries the status text.

There is a confirmed, dev-data-only bug worth flagging so it isn't mistaken for a real defect: both seeded products use `https://example.com/images/...` thumbnail URLs, and every page that renders them throws a repeated CSP violation, since the admin's `img-src` policy only allows `self`, `data:`, the API origin, and Google Maps domains. This makes the two seeded demo products render as blank gray boxes everywhere in the admin — a bad first impression for anyone exploring the seeded dev environment, though real merchant-uploaded images served from the API origin would not hit this. Mobile list view reflows acceptably with no overflow issues observed.

### Product Create Wizard

The wizard is a well-structured three-step flow (Basics → Pricing & Inventory → Organization) with state fully centralized in `useProductForm.ts`, and cross-step validation on final submit correctly maps errors back to the earliest offending step. This was verified live: submitting with no price or SKU while parked on the Organization step correctly bounced the wizard back to Pricing & Inventory with the right fields highlighted.

**The most severe finding in this entire audit's Products slice, and one of the most severe in the whole report, is here: the wizard's sticky footer silently swallows clicks on content that scrolls underneath it.** `ProductForm.tsx` renders the Back/Cancel/Duplicate/Next-or-Save footer as a `sticky bottom-0` div positioned after the step's content, with no `z-index` set. Because it has no explicit stacking context, it paints on top of any earlier sibling it visually overlaps — exactly what happens for most of the scroll range on a tall page. This was proven, not inferred: scrolling the Organization step so the "+ Add variants" toggle sits at the bottom of the viewport, its own bounding rectangle and the footer's bounding rectangle fully overlap, and `document.elementFromPoint` at the button's own center returns a different element — the footer itself, not the control underneath it. A screenshot of this state shows the "+ Add variants" card completely hidden behind the opaque footer bar.

This is not limited to Variants — it will swallow clicks on anything that scrolls into the bottom ~66–88px band: the Attributes/FAQs toggle links, the "Search engine listing" details summary, the Status combobox, "+ Add block" under Additional Info, Additional Info field inputs, and the Collections checkbox tree when it's long enough to reach that band. It reproduces identically in Advanced mode (the exact same markup, same missing `z-index`) and is, if anything, worse on mobile, where the Media dropzone's own hint text was already visibly clipped by the footer on Step 1 alone. This makes a meaningful fraction of both wizard steps 2 and 3, and the entire advanced single-page editor's lower content, non-interactive at very common scroll positions, with zero visual indication anything is wrong — the footer just looks like a normal footer bar.

A closely related, second confirmed bug: the validation-failure toast visually covers the wizard's own primary action button. Toasts render in a fixed bottom-right corner — the same corner where the wizard's Next/Create button lives — and measuring both elements' rectangles directly on a failed submit shows the button is entirely inside the toast's bounding box. So the exact moment a merchant is told "fix the highlighted fields to continue," their own retry click on Next/Create is likely to land on the toast instead, for the toast's roughly three-second lifetime. This shares the same root cause as the sticky-footer bug: nothing in this app's overlay stack accounts for the wizard's own persistent bottom-right action button.

A third, smaller finding: the Stepper's "completed" checkmark does not mean a step is actually valid. Each step's status is derived purely from whether it's been visited, and `handleNext()` only validates the Title field before advancing off Step 1 — Step 2 (Pricing & Inventory) has zero forward-navigation gating despite having two required fields. It's possible to advance off Step 2 with Price and SKU both empty and showing red "required" text on screen, landing on Step 3 with Step 2 marked complete, even though final submit will bounce the user straight back with the same errors. Final-submit validation does still catch this, so it isn't a hard blocker, but it undermines the Stepper's basic promise.

Working correctly and worth noting: advancing from Step 1 with no image attached is allowed by design (images are only required at final submit); the wizard correctly picks up the shop's `productEditorMode` to default Variants/Attributes/FAQs open state; and the wizard correctly renders through `PageShell variant="wide"` with no dead space at 1440px, matching the fix CLAUDE.md documents for this exact page. One minor code-level finding: both `products/new/page.tsx` and `products/[id]/edit/page.tsx` wrap `<ProductForm/>` in their own `PageShell`, while `ProductForm.tsx` also wraps its own return value in a second `PageShell` — cosmetically harmless, but a clear instance of the shell being applied twice, once by the page and once by the component it renders.

### Variants, Images, Attributes/FAQs, Checkout Add-ons

Variant option and value entry (comma-splitting on Enter, dedup, per-value chip removal) works correctly. Several scripted interactions here silently no-op'd during testing, and root-causing them led directly to the sticky-footer bug above — this is the same defect surfacing in Variants, not a separate one. `VariantEditModal` is a well-designed, portal-based modal, and image assignment inside it correctly tracks by URL rather than id, supporting a same-session freshly-uploaded image with no defect found on read-through. Worth flagging as a stale-documentation item: CLAUDE.md explains the portal's existence by noting `ProductForm` used to render a real `<form>` element that a nested modal-form couldn't sit inside — but `ProductForm.tsx` no longer renders a `<form>` at all (every button is `type="button"`), so the documented justification for the portal is now confirmed genuinely stale, exactly as CLAUDE.md itself flags but has not yet acted on.

The image gallery (multi-image, drag-to-reorder, featured-image badge on position 0) renders and behaves correctly. There is one real accessibility gap: `ImageDropzone.tsx`'s label has no `htmlFor` attribute and the file input has no matching `id` — contrast with `Input.tsx`, used throughout the same wizard, which correctly generates and pairs a `useId()`-based id/htmlFor. The "Image" label here has no programmatic association with the upload control for screen readers; the click-to-browse behavior only works because the outer wrapper has its own click handler, not because of the label.

Attributes and FAQs are a clean, reusable, per-product opt-in feature (a native `<details>` element, no JS accordion library) and are fully wired end to end on the storefront, correctly gated per-product. Checkout add-ons are likewise fully wired end to end: the Pricing step's toggle sets the flag, and both the PDP's "You might also want" section and the separate checkout add-on popup filter on it correctly, excluding the product being viewed from its own suggestions.

Missing versus Shopify: no product metafields, no product bundles or "frequently bought together" beyond the single flat checkout-addon flag, no 3D or video media support in the gallery (image-only, confirmed by the dropzone's `accept="image/*"`), and no quick-edit grid — every edit requires opening the full wizard or advanced page. There is no product reviews or star-rating feature anywhere in the codebase, confirmed by grep and cross-checked against CLAUDE.md's own exhaustive module roster, which has no reviews module — a real, notable gap relative to most e-commerce platforms.

UAE/Dukany-specific gaps: no Arabic-language product fields (name and description are single-locale, with no equivalent `nameAr`/`descriptionAr`) — consistent with the platform's documented, deliberate scope cut on i18n, not a regression, but still a real competitive gap. There's also no WhatsApp catalog sharing or sync; the only WhatsApp touchpoint on a product is a narrow "contact us to order" link used exclusively in the cart-disabled "contact to order" mode, and it pre-fills only a product name, not a rich card, image, or price.

### Product Edit

Editing correctly opens on Step 1 with all three steps pre-marked visited. Live-tested against the seeded "Red Rose Bouquet": all existing data — tags, collection membership, Additional Info blocks — loaded and rendered correctly. No data-loading defects found. The sticky-footer-overlap bug documented above applies identically here since it's the same shared component.

## Collections / Categories

Note on naming: per a 2026-08-09 rename documented in CLAUDE.md, "Collections" in this codebase is the product taxonomy tree, and "Templates" is the separate marketing-groupings feature — both are covered here since they sit under the same Products sub-nav.

Collections are fully functional in live testing: creating a new collection produced a correct auto-derived slug, a success toast, and immediate appearance in both the tree list and the product wizard's collection checkbox tree on the very next load. The nested-tree rendering, with vertical guide lines and native HTML5 drag-to-reorder, is well implemented, and empty/loading states are present. The create modal correctly uses the shared `Button.loading` convention.

Real gaps versus the sibling Template model and versus Shopify: a Collection has no SEO metadata at all — no meta title or description equivalent, unlike a Product, which has both — so a collection's storefront page SEO can only ever fall back to its name and description. There's also no active/visibility toggle: Template has a real `isActive` boolean that hides it from the storefront without deleting it, but Collection has no equivalent — the only way to stop a Collection from appearing on the storefront is to delete it outright, which also strips it from every product's assignment. And there's no product-count column on the Collections list, whereas Templates shows exactly this — a merchant browsing Collections has no at-a-glance way to see how many products are in "Flowers" versus "Gifts" without opening the product list and filtering.

On mobile, the Products sub-nav tab bar (Products / Collections / Templates / Discounts / Gift Cards) overflows and visibly truncates with no fade or chevron hinting that it's horizontally scrollable; it is scrollable, but nothing signals it, so a mobile user could easily miss that Discounts and Gift Cards exist as tabs at all.

Templates are functionally solid: type-switching between Manual, Rule-based, and Collection-group modes works correctly, title-to-slug derivation works, and the empty state renders correctly. Two confirmed convention violations here: the form hardcodes `max-w-3xl` on its own top-level `<form>` while sitting inside a default, uncapped `PageShell` — a screenshot-confirmed instance of the exact "arbitrary narrow box with dead space" problem CLAUDE.md documents having fixed once already elsewhere, with the entire form crammed into roughly the left 57% of a 1440px viewport. And both the save and delete buttons hand-roll a disabled-plus-text-swap loading state instead of using the shared `Button.loading` prop that CLAUDE.md documents as mandatory — notably, the sibling Collection modal in the same feature area gets this right, making it an internal inconsistency within the same feature rather than a stray legacy pattern. Templates also has no bulk actions, no CSV import/export, and no drag-and-drop reordering — membership ordering uses typed numeric inputs in a table instead, a real UX asymmetry against the closely related Collections page a merchant will use side by side.

## Discounts

Discount CRUD is solid: a percentage code and an auto-apply discount were both created live and round-tripped correctly, with the list correctly rendering an "Auto" or "Code" pill next to each discount's name.

There is a screenshot-confirmed layout bug worth filing: the discount form modal's Active toggle is the last item in the scrollable field block, immediately followed by the sticky Cancel/Create button row. In code mode the form is short enough that this never collides, but in auto mode the extra "Applies to" product/collection multi-select pushes total content height past what fits, and the sticky footer visually overlaps and obscures the Active toggle — a merchant creating an auto-discount cannot reliably see or click it without first discovering that the form scrolls, since the sticky footer looks like the natural end of the form.

There's also a clear convention violation: the discount list's Active/Inactive control is a custom bordered pill using red for the inactive state, not the shared `Toggle` component — directly contradicting CLAUDE.md's documented decision to move the whole app away from red-for-off specifically because it read as "broken" rather than "not configured yet." This page still uses that exact anti-pattern.

Structurally, there is no discount-combination or stacking model at all: neither the backend constants nor the frontend types have any "combines with" or priority concept, so a cart can only ever carry one code, and there's no way for an auto-discount to stack with a code discount either. This is a real gap against Shopify, which has explicit combination classes with a combines-with matrix. There's also no buy-X-get-Y or BOGO discount type — the type union is only percentage, fixed amount, and free shipping — so a common flower-shop promo like "buy two bouquets, get one free" simply cannot be built. The only quantity-based trigger is a minimum dollar amount, not a minimum item count, which Shopify's discount editor supports.

**The most severe finding in this section, and one of the most severe in the whole audit, is that auto-apply discount pricing is cosmetic only — and the gap is worse than the platform's own documentation admits.** CLAUDE.md is upfront that order-total redemption of an auto-discount isn't wired at checkout yet, which is a documented, deliberate scope cut and not itself something to relitigate. But tracing the actual storefront code shows the problem goes further: the *displayed* discounted price is never even passed into the cart. The product detail page computes an auto-discounted price and renders it as a struck-through sale price, and the product card does the same — but the function that builds a cart line item uses the raw, undiscounted price, not the computed discounted one. A shopper sees "15% off, 85 instead of 100" on the PDP or product card, but the moment they click Add to Cart, the line item is silently seeded at the full price — with the visible discount having been exactly what drove the purchase decision. This is a customer-facing bait-and-switch bug, not merely an incomplete feature, and nothing in the admin UI's auto-discount copy ("Applies automatically to every matching cart, no code needed") hints to a merchant that the price quoted to shoppers isn't what actually gets charged.

## Gift Cards

Manual admin-issued gift cards work correctly: issuing a card produced an instantly-visible list entry with a correctly grouped code, and balance/value/status all correct.

**The most significant finding in this section: there is no admin UI anywhere to configure a sellable gift-card product, despite the feature being fully built and working end to end everywhere else.** The backend fully supports it — the product DTO defines `isGiftCard` and `giftCardDenominations` — and the storefront fully consumes both fields across the product detail page, the cart, checkout, and the theme-driven product grid section. But a direct search of the entire admin app for `isGiftCard` returns zero matches, and a full read of the Pricing step of the product wizard confirms there is no gift-card toggle or denomination-list editor anywhere in any of the three wizard steps. The only way a merchant could ever set `product.isGiftCard = true` is a raw API call — the admin panel gives no path to it at all, despite the storefront purchase flow being fully built and demonstrably working.

There's also a convention violation of the same class documented elsewhere in this report: the gift card list's Enable/Disable action is a plain text link, not an icon button, which is exactly the deprecated treatment CLAUDE.md documents the rest of the app having converged away from — Gift Cards is the one page in this slice still using it, inconsistent with its sibling Discounts and Ingredients pages in the same section.

Missing versus Shopify: there's no recipient-email field on the manual-issuance panel at all — the backend's own comment on this notes the admin "relays it to the customer however they normally would (phone, WhatsApp)," meaning there's no send-by-email action for a manually issued card, unlike Shopify's issuance flow (send now or schedule, recipient name and email, custom note). There's also no gift-card design or branding — the storefront purchase flow generates a fixed HTML email template that isn't merchant-customizable — and no search, filter, or pagination on the admin gift card list, which becomes unusable for a shop with hundreds of issued cards.

On mobile, this list has the same column-cutoff problem documented elsewhere: the Purchased-By and Enable/Disable columns are entirely off-screen with no scroll affordance, making the only row action on this page unreachable on a phone without undiscoverable horizontal scrolling.

## Inventory (Ingredients, Stock, Scan to Stock, Movement History)

Note on naming: "Inventory" in the admin nav is exclusively the ingredients and raw-materials stock section, entirely separate from the product catalog at Products.

The full flow was live-tested: an ingredient was created, an outlet was selected, Adjust Stock was opened and correctly rejected a submission with no reason selected before accepting one with a reason and applying a +20 change that updated the list live, and a low-stock threshold was set successfully. Transfer Stock is functional but structurally unusable for this seeded shop, which has only one outlet — the "To branch" field has no valid option, yet the action renders unconditionally regardless of outlet count; worth hiding or disabling when a shop has fewer than two outlets.

A real inconsistency against the product creation flow: the ingredient form has no initial-stock or low-stock-threshold field at all — stock can only be set after the ingredient is saved, via a separate per-outlet Adjust Stock action. The product wizard, by contrast, shows an inline quantity-by-branch table right at creation time. A merchant creating twenty new ingredients must save each one, then open Adjust Stock twenty separate times to seed opening stock, with no bulk or inline path. Related: the ingredient form has no `trackInventory` toggle at all, despite the field being fully supported end to end on the backend and by CSV import — every manually created ingredient silently defaults to tracked with no way to change that from the create or edit modal.

There is no merchant-facing low-stock notification of any kind. The only low-stock signal anywhere in the admin UI is a manual "Low stock only" filter checkbox on the Ingredients list and the same red badge on the Products list — there is no email, dashboard banner, or digest when an item actually crosses its threshold, in contrast to Shopify's low-stock smart collections and flow alerts, and in contrast to this same codebase's own customer-facing back-in-stock notification system, which has no merchant-side equivalent.

The mobile table-overflow problem documented throughout this report recurs here across three separate pages, and is worth naming as a pattern rather than three isolated bugs: on Movement History at 390px, the Quantity and Timestamp columns — arguably the two most important columns in a movement log — are completely invisible with no scroll affordance; on Gift Cards, the Purchased-By and action columns are cut off the same way; and on Discounts, Usage, Validity, Active, and both row actions are all cut off, with the section's own tab bar clipping "Discounts" and "Gift Cards" out of view on top of that.

Scan to Stock renders cleanly at both viewports, with the settings accordion (exclude/include keywords, default outlet, unmatched-item behavior) opening correctly and the scan button correctly disabled until a file is chosen. A full source read confirms solid commit-time validation — it blocks committing a recipe-based product, blocks a variant product missing a variant selection, and blocks a new product or ingredient missing required fields. Ingredient Categories is a minimal, functional CRUD page with a clean empty state and no issues found.

## Outlets / Branches

The branches list renders cleanly at both viewports, with a status badge, a "(manual)" annotation when an outlet is force-closed, and correctly icon-only Edit/Delete actions. Delete is gated behind a native `confirm()` rather than the shared `Modal` — a minor inconsistency with the rest of the app's modal-driven confirm flows, not a functional bug.

At 390px, the table's Location, Delivery, Pickup, and Edit/Delete columns are pushed off-screen with no visual cue that they're reachable by horizontal swipe — the table is technically scrollable, but a merchant on a phone could reasonably conclude the list has no edit affordance at all, since the icon buttons that provide it are exactly what's hidden.

The outlet edit page's six-tab layout (Basic Information, Address, Delivery, Delivery Area, Pickup, QR) responds correctly on mobile, collapsing to a stacked nav above the content. Two navigation labels are easy to conflate here: the **Delivery** tab holds shop-wide payment-method, hours, and prep-time settings that are explicitly not per-outlet, while the actually-per-outlet named delivery-zone configuration lives one tab over under **Delivery Area**. A first-pass search for a "New zone" button under Delivery correctly finds nothing, because it isn't there — a merchant is likely to make the same first click before finding it under Delivery Area.

### Delivery Zones and the Google Maps rewrite

The delivery-zone map itself works: opening "New Zone" rendered a fully tiled, interactive UAE basemap at both viewports, with no fallback error text and no console errors during that test. The radius slider and pin-drag interactions match the documented design.

However, **the separate outlet-address map, which shares the identical loader and API key, failed live in a different test run with a real console error: `BillingNotEnabledMapError`.** The map rendered as a flat gray box with only the marker pin floating on it — no tiles, roads, or labels. Since the two components share an identical loader and key, this isn't a code difference between them; it points at the Google Cloud project behind the shared key being at or near a billing or quota boundary, intermittently. This is the single most concrete, actionable infrastructure finding in this audit: checking the Google Cloud Console billing status for the configured Maps API key would confirm or rule this out directly, and it's worth doing before this reaches production, since it sits on the exact page a merchant uses to pin an outlet's own delivery-radius center.

### Business Settings

The Business Information tab follows the card-and-grid convention well and is responsive at mobile. One soft convention tension: the Notification Settings card uses `Checkbox` for three independent binary preferences rather than `Toggle`, which is arguably a violation of the documented "Toggle, not Checkbox, for binary state fields" rule, though the same pattern recurs elsewhere in this file family, suggesting it's an established, if debatable, house style rather than an isolated slip.

Store Configuration has the two most severe concrete bugs found in Settings. First, the Default Delivery Fee field displays `0.000000000000000000000000000000` instead of `0` — confirmed directly against the backend, which returns the raw, untrimmed `DECIMAL(65,30)` string for this field. CLAUDE.md documents `trimDecimal()` as the established fix for exactly this class of bug elsewhere in the codebase, but `shop.defaultDeliveryFee` was evidently missed on both the backend service and the frontend input binding — this is the same defect class as the Customer Detail order-total bug documented above, now confirmed in a second, independent location, which suggests a broader audit of every unannotated decimal field passed straight through to a response is warranted rather than fixing these two spots in isolation.

Second, a factually wrong disclaimer: the "Product Display Orientation" setting is captioned "Saved as a preference only. No storefront exists yet to apply it to," which is simply false — the storefront's collection page reads this exact field to drive its grid-versus-list rendering. This disclaimer predates the storefront's build-out and was never removed.

A third finding is a misleading disclaimer that lumps a live feature in with a dead one: the "Engagement" card shows two checkboxes, "WhatsApp floating button enabled" and "Birthday discount enabled," under one shared caption stating neither is connected to anything yet. That's accurate for the birthday-discount flag, which genuinely has zero consumer beyond database passthrough — but the WhatsApp floating-button flag is fully wired and directly gates the real, customer-facing floating chat button on the storefront. Telling a merchant this toggle "does nothing" when it controls a shipped, customer-visible feature will cause merchants to either distrust a working feature or skip turning it on. The same page has a broader pattern of using `Checkbox` for standalone binary status fields (pre-orders, order confirmation requirement, external delivery, ASAP delivery, delivery calendar, the WhatsApp button, birthday discounts) rather than `Toggle` — notable because this exact page is the one CLAUDE.md cites as the *legitimate* example of Checkbox's proper multi-select use case, making these newer additions read as drift from the page's own established precedent.

### Payment Providers

Layout is solid at both viewports. The most significant finding: **Nomod is presented as a fully equal, functional choice next to Stripe with no indication anywhere in the UI that it is a structural stub.** The card-processor radio group lets a merchant select Nomod — the first-listed, and so arguably default-looking, option — enter API credentials, and save, with zero warning. The backend provider unconditionally throws an internal server error from `createCheckoutSession`, meaning every real checkout attempt would fail outright. A merchant who picks Nomod would have every checkout 500 at runtime with no warning anywhere in settings that this would happen — a real revenue and trust risk, not a cosmetic one.

Telr and PayTabs are entirely absent from the admin UI, not merely present-but-disabled. Both have real backend stub providers and platform-level fallback credentials documented in the codebase, but no merchant can even see they exist — not even as a grayed-out "coming soon" row. For a UAE and Gulf-focused SaaS explicitly benchmarked against Dukany in this audit, Telr and PayTabs are two of the most common regional gateways, and their complete invisibility is a concrete competitive gap. PayPal, Tabby, and Tamara — the genuinely implemented independent providers — render correctly with their documented credential fields and correct framing, and no issues were found there. Cash on Delivery's single visibility toggle works as described.

### WhatsApp

WhatsApp configuration is split across three separate controls in two different tabs with no single surface tying them together: the WhatsApp number and country-code selector live on Business Information, the "Notify Customers via WhatsApp" checkbox lives on the same page in a different card, the WhatsApp Business API credentials (Phone Number ID and Access Token) live in their own card at the bottom of that same page, and the floating-button toggle lives on the separate Store Configuration tab. Nothing here is broken, but a merchant trying to fully turn on WhatsApp has to hunt across two tabs and three unrelated-looking controls. The credentials card itself is well built — masked display of saved values, a clear one-line explanation of where to get them, and an honest fallback note that notifications fall back to a console log without them — but compared to a typical Dukany-style guided WhatsApp Business API onboarding, this is a bare paste-the-values form with no guided setup.

### Staff and Branch Roles

This is one of the strongest-tested areas in the audit. The full branch-specific-roles flow was exercised live rather than just read: creating a new branch role surfaced exactly the ten fixed permissions the platform documents, with the restrict-only guarantee stated inline in the modal's own copy, and the role saved with zero console errors. The new-staff-account modal correctly exposes all four roles and includes a "set a password directly" escape hatch alongside the invite-by-email flow. The assign-branch-role modal correctly populated its three dropdowns and did not error even when the only assignable staff member was the shop's own admin account — which is the documented, intended use case (restricting a shop-wide admin at one specific branch), not an edge case that should have been blocked. No console errors or failed requests were observed across the entire live create-role-then-assign flow. One real gap: this seeded shop only has an admin user, so what a logged-in branch, order-manager, or viewer account actually sees under an active restriction could not be exercised in this pass — flagged as untested, not verified working.

### Jobs

The Failed Jobs admin view renders correctly at both viewports with a proper empty state and correctly icon-only Retry/Dismiss actions. No jobs were in a failed state during this pass, so those actions were not exercised against a real failure.

## Theme Builder

The builder is a fixed three-panel desktop layout — a section/settings tree on the left, the live preview iframe in the center, and a settings panel on the right — with none of the three panels carrying any responsive breakpoint. This is confirmed structurally, not just observed: the two fixed sidebar widths alone exceed a 390px viewport with nothing to collapse them, so the builder is a desktop-only tool today, by design or by omission, with nothing in the code suggesting a mobile treatment was ever planned.

Undo and redo are real and correctly implemented — a twenty-entry capped history stack with keyboard shortcuts that are correctly skipped while focus sits in an editable field — which is worth stating plainly since it directly contradicts an assumption this audit started with. The section tree (Header, an ordered list of toggleable sections with drag handles, Footer) renders and behaves correctly, with Undo/Redo and Discard/Publish buttons all present and correctly state-gated.

**The single most severe bug in the entire Theme Builder, and one of the most severe in this whole report, is a hard crash: clicking the "Product cards" settings category takes down the entire builder.** Every other category up through and including this one — Logo and favicon, Colors, Typography, Page layout, Animations, Badges, Buttons, Cart, Drawers, Icons, Input fields, Popovers and modals, Prices — rendered correctly with populated forms and a live-updating preview. Product Cards instead throws a `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'trim')` inside the shared `ColorPicker` component, and the error escapes the app's local error boundary entirely, replacing the whole three-panel editor with Next's generic "Something went wrong" screen. This reproduced deterministically on two clean reloads.

The root cause traces cleanly through the actual code: `ColorPicker`'s hex-normalization helper calls `.trim()` unconditionally on every render with no guard for `undefined`, and the three color fields Product Cards renders through it are missing on this particular theme's persisted configuration because that theme was created before those fields existed in the schema. The backend does have a backfill mechanism specifically for this class of gap — but it's a shallow merge that only deep-merges one category, `collectionPage`, by name, because that's the one category previously confirmed to hit this exact failure mode and fixed. Every other category, including Product Cards, still gets whatever incomplete object the old theme row happens to have, verbatim. The backfill code's own comment explicitly predicts this — "the same latent gap could exist elsewhere; flagged in the PR rather than blanket-fixed without the same live confirmation this one got" — and this audit has now live-confirmed it for a second category. Given the global settings schema has grown by eighteen categories over several phases and only one has ever been confirmed and fixed this way, every other category should be treated as suspect until verified the same way; the fix pattern already exists in the codebase (deep-merge every category the way `collectionPage` already is, or move to a generic recursive default-merge rather than one hand-enumerated exception) but hasn't been applied broadly. Any real merchant whose theme predates a field added to any of the eighteen categories will hit this exact crash the instant they open that category.

Custom CSS is a real, wired feature rather than a placeholder: a 1500-character limit with a live counter, client-side warnings for disallowed constructs like `<script>` or `@import` that are hard-enforced server-side, and it reads and writes through the same live-preview channel every other setting uses.

### Layout mode (13 legacy categories)

All thirteen categories — Home tab, Menu, Homepage layout, Top bar layout, Header size, Footer layout, Footer size, Product page layout, Cart layout, Checkout layout, Icon style, Button shape, Button fill — opened cleanly with no crashes. This mode's live-preview wiring, documented in CLAUDE.md as only having been added on 2026-08-16, was confirmed intact: the preview frame posts a debounced update whenever the legacy theme state changes, symmetric to the newer system's own channel.

### Drag-and-drop reliability — the historically reported "snap back" bug

This was the most rigorously tested item in this section given its four-times-reported history. Using raw mouse events to drag a section row past the activation threshold and drop it in a new position, the reorder took effect correctly in the sidebar — and, critically, **a full hard page reload afterward showed the identical new order, not a reversion to the original.** This directly and personally reproduces the fix as holding under real mouse-driven interaction, not merely as claimed in a changelog: the reorder function calls an immediate save right after the state update, and that immediate save was verified to survive a genuine reload. The same immediate-save behavior was separately verified for the Home tab's preset-arrangement feature, which also survived a hard reload with an identical section list before and after. One side effect worth flagging to whoever owns the test fixture: both of these tests permanently mutated the seeded theme's actual persisted section arrangement, since every action auto-saves by design, and no attempt was made to restore the original arrangement afterward.

### Live preview sync, page switcher, in-preview editing

Both the newer sections system and the legacy layout system were confirmed live-syncing to the preview correctly, with no dropped messages or console errors observed across the full category pass. Navigation was not found to break in-preview selection — the preview page switcher correctly navigated the iframe between home, collection, and product-page previews, each rendering real, correctly themed content pulled from real backend data, and a subsequent click-to-select test on a freshly navigated page worked correctly end to end. Clicking an editable element inside the preview correctly produced a selection outline and floating toolbar in the iframe, auto-expanded and highlighted the corresponding row in the sidebar tree, and correctly swapped the right-hand settings panel to that block's real fields — confirming the full round trip from preview click through to the settings UI. In-preview drag-and-drop reordering (as opposed to the sidebar's own drag, which was fully verified above) was not exercised live in this pass and should be treated as less thoroughly verified, though it shares the same underlying save-immediately code path already confirmed correct via the sidebar test.

The toolbar's settings search box was found to be genuinely broken for any category with nested field groups, confirmed on Colors: typing a query that matches only one field's label does not hide any of the sibling fields, because the filter only operates at one DOM level and the Colors category nests all of its fields two levels deep inside a single wrapping card, whose aggregate text content happens to contain the query term regardless of which specific field matched. This fails silently — nothing errors, the box accepts input and looks functional, it simply doesn't filter anything in this category.

One cosmetic oddity worth a design pass: the Home tab's explanatory banner about the sections builder contains a small, oddly clipped pill-shaped element reading just "To" immediately before the sentence continues — this reads like a truncated or under-sized inline component rather than intentional copy.

### Comparison to Shopify and Dukany

Concrete gaps against Shopify's Online Store 2.0 editor: no app-block extensibility model at all; no theme duplication — the theme library only offers Edit and Delete per theme, and creating a new theme always starts blank even though the backend has cloning machinery already built for its own default-theme seeding, just not exposed as a "duplicate this theme" action; no section groups; and no metaobject-driven sections, though that's a reasonable omission for a florist-vertical SaaS rather than a general platform. Draft/publish versioning does exist and is close to parity, with a real published configuration distinct from the current draft, but there's no named version history or "save as new theme" snapshot beyond the single current draft.

For the UAE market specifically: there is no RTL support anywhere in the theme builder, no Arabic-language preview toggle, and no mirrored-layout preview — consistent with the platform's documented, deliberate scope cut on i18n rather than a surprise regression, but worth naming explicitly for a tool aimed at UAE and Gulf merchants where a direct competitor supports RTL theming natively.

### Accessibility

The sidebar's section drag is keyboard-operable in principle, since it's wired with the standard keyboard-sensor pattern for its drag library, though this wasn't verified live. The in-preview drag is unambiguously mouse and pointer only, with no keyboard equivalent — reordering a block from inside the live preview itself is not accessible by keyboard at all, only the sidebar's parallel path is. Nothing in the UI visually distinguishes which of the two parallel theming systems — the newer sections builder or the older layout mode — a given setting belongs to beyond which top-level tab a merchant happens to be looking under, which is a real, if soft, discoverability problem baked into having two systems by design.

## Storefront

Testing here required first publishing the seeded shop and enabling outlet delivery and pickup through the real admin API, since the shop started in an unpublished state where the catalog and collection endpoints correctly 404 by design — this mirrors exactly the state change a merchant makes via "Publish your store" and is noted here as a data-state change, not a code edit.

### Home Page, Header, Footer

The homepage falls back correctly to the classic hero layout when `homepageLayout` is set to a value the dispatch doesn't recognize, rendering a hero, a featured-collections tile row, and a plain product grid with no console errors from React itself — the only errors observed were CSP rejections of the seeded products' fake external placeholder image URLs, which is the CSP correctly doing its job against non-resolvable seed data rather than a real bug.

There is a real, confirmed visual bug in the persistent header, present on every single page: the shop's short tagline text renders at a hero-sized 32px in a serif display font, crammed into the header row next to the small brand name, and gets truncated mid-word — to "Free shipping over 20…" on desktop and to just "Free…" on mobile. The element itself is a bare, unstyled span with no explicit size class, so it's inheriting an ambient heading-preset rule from the shop's published theme rather than being deliberately sized. Because this sits in the persistent header, it's visible and broken on every page of the storefront, making it one of the more consequential small findings in this audit purely by virtue of its reach.

The header and footer chrome otherwise behave correctly: the icon-only header controls (search, cart with badge, account, order tracking) all carry proper `aria-label`s, the cart icon correctly hides itself entirely when a shop has disabled its store cart, and the footer's graceful single-column degradation when a shop has no social links or policy pages configured is working as documented, not a bug.

### Collection Page

Renders correctly with a breadcrumb, a filter and sort bar (category, sort order, an optional price-range slider, and an in-collection search box), a result count, and the product grid — a solid, Shopify-parity feature set for a collection page, with sort options spanning best-selling, newest, oldest, alphabetical, and price ascending or descending. No console errors beyond the same seed-data CSP issue, and the layout collapses correctly to mobile.

### Product Detail Page

Confirmed working: breadcrumb, an active auto-discount rendered correctly as a strikethrough original price next to a highlighted discounted price, a stock indicator, delivery and pickup availability badges, a quantity stepper, side-by-side Add to Cart and Buy Now buttons, an optional 200-character item note field, a "You might also want" checkout-addon upsell row, a trust strip, and collapsible Care Instructions and Delivery accordion sections. Add-to-cart correctly incremented the header's cart badge.

The audit brief specifically asked whether the PDP shows star ratings or reviews with no real feature behind them — it does not. A full grep across the storefront for review, rating, star, and wishlist terminology found no matches tied to a real product-review system anywhere; the only near-hits are an unrelated, merchant-authored testimonial theme block and generic substring matches. There is no dead or misleading ratings widget to flag here — the feature is simply and completely absent, which is worth stating precisely given how common a half-built ratings UI is on platforms at this stage.

Missing versus Shopify beyond reviews: no wishlist or save-for-later anywhere in the codebase, no "recently viewed" tracking, and no back-in-stock notification beyond a plain email-capture form with no SMS or WhatsApp option, despite this being a UAE-focused product where WhatsApp notification would be a natural fit. On the positive side, the cross-sell upsell row and the auto-discount strikethrough pricing are both genuinely well-executed, arguably ahead of many out-of-the-box Shopify themes.

### Cart and Checkout

The cart page is clean and functional — line items with thumbnails, quantity steppers, a remove link, a promo-code field, and a correct subtotal and total. There is no express-checkout or wallet-pay button (Apple Pay, Google Pay) anywhere in cart or checkout, and no guest one-click option — a real, named Shopify-parity gap, confirmed by reading the full payment-method roster in the codebase, which has no wallet-pay integration of any kind.

**Checkout is where this audit's single most severe finding lives, and it's a P0: every text input and textarea on the checkout form renders invisible, near-black-on-near-black text for any visitor whose OS or browser prefers dark mode.** This was confirmed by measuring computed styles directly, not just by looking at a screenshot — every field (name, phone, email, the delivery address search box, full address, emirate, area, delivery date, time slot, notes, promo code, gift card) resolves to a text color and background color with a contrast ratio close to 1:1, meaning genuinely unreadable, invisible text the moment a visitor types. The root cause traces precisely: the storefront's global stylesheet already contains a deliberate, previously-shipped fix removing a blanket dark-mode media-query override from the root CSS variables, on the explicit reasoning that a visitor's OS preference has no business overriding a shop's own branding — but that fix was never propagated to two separate shared field-style constant files that still hard-code literal Tailwind dark-mode utility classes, which key off the same OS media query independently of the CSS-variable fix. One of these files sets a dark background with no explicit text color at all, and is used throughout the entire checkout form; the other affects the address textarea used both in checkout and in the account address book, in a file where the sibling text-input style was correctly migrated to the fixed pattern but the textarea style was missed. This is not a hypothetical — it's the literal reason a scripted checkout submission during this audit looked like it had filled nothing: the fields weren't empty, the typed text was simply invisible against its own background. Any real shopper on a dark-mode OS or browser — an unremarkable and large fraction of mobile users — cannot see what they're typing anywhere on checkout, or in the address book's address field. This is narrowly scoped and cheaply fixable (the same text-color fix already applied correctly in the one file that got it right needs to be applied to the two that didn't, or the leftover dark-mode classes simply deleted to match the rest of the already-fixed codebase), and it should be at or near the top of any punch list from this audit given that it silently blocks the platform's core revenue path for an entire class of visitors.

A second, related checkout finding: delivery orders hard-require map-picked coordinates, and submitting with a typed free-text address but no map interaction correctly returns a clear error from the backend — but the form gives no earlier signal that typing an address by hand won't be sufficient. Nothing marks the map or search step as required before submit, so a shopper who fills the address field by hand only discovers the requirement after a full submit attempt fails.

A third finding, likely specific to this environment's API key configuration but worth documenting as a real code-level gap regardless: the checkout map component only handles a Maps script failing to load, not a script that loads successfully but then fails at runtime — which is exactly what a billing or quota error produces. During testing, this surfaced as a raw, Google-branded "this page can't load Google Maps correctly" dialog rendered inside the app's own map box, rather than the app's own designed fallback message, meaning any future billing, key, or quota problem in production would show shoppers Google's own unstyled error rather than something the platform controls.

On the positive side for UAE-market fit: Cash on Delivery is present and correctly selectable, and the Emirate selector correctly lists all seven Emirates. There is, however, no Makani number field anywhere in the codebase — a real, named gap against Dukany-style local competitors, which commonly support Makani as a precise UAE addressing field alongside GPS pin-drop. And there is no Arabic or RTL toggle for shoppers anywhere on the storefront — consistent with the platform's documented, deliberate scope cut on i18n, not a surprise, but worth stating as a confirmed and complete absence for a UAE-market product.

### Account and Order Confirmation/Tracking

Login and registration pages are clean and correctly labeled, and their text fields are readable — they use the one shared field-style file that was correctly migrated away from the dark-mode bug described above, in direct contrast to checkout. A direct API check confirms registration itself works correctly end to end; a scripted UI registration attempt in this pass didn't clearly succeed or fail, but the likely explanation is a duplication artifact in the test script's own field-filling logic rather than a real product defect, and this is flagged as inconclusive rather than asserted as a bug. The address book page shares the same invisible-text bug documented under Checkout, since its address textarea pulls from the same unmigrated style file.

Order confirmation and tracking pages are well built by source reading: the confirmation page's tracking-token callout is a genuinely good pattern for guest checkout, and the tracking page correctly distinguishes a shopper's own verified order from anonymous guest tracking, with that distinction enforced server-side rather than inferred client-side. Neither could be exercised against a real, fully completed order in this pass, since checkout itself could not be driven to full completion given the two findings above — this section is verified by source reading and page-chrome screenshots rather than a live completed order.

### Accessibility summary

Icon-only header controls consistently carry `aria-label`s, quantity steppers correctly label increase and decrease, and variant option buttons use `aria-pressed` correctly. The one clear, severe accessibility failure on the storefront is the checkout dark-mode text bug documented above — a hard blocker for any low-vision-adjacent or simply dark-mode-preferring user, not a cosmetic issue, since there is no visual feedback at all that a field even has a value in it. The storefront has no loading-skeleton convention analogous to the admin's design system — every page shows a plain "Loading…" text fallback — a smaller, purely cosmetic gap by comparison.

## Priority Ranking

The following ten items are ranked by impact to a merchant actually trying to run a real flower and gift delivery business on this platform in the UAE today — weighted toward anything that silently loses revenue, silently misrepresents a price to a customer, or blocks a core, everyday workflow, over cosmetic or long-tail feature gaps.

1. **Auto-apply discounts show a price to shoppers that is never actually charged.** The PDP and product card display a correctly computed, struck-through discounted price, but the cart line item is built from the full undiscounted price. This is a direct pricing-integrity and customer-trust failure on real money, not a cosmetic bug, and it's live right now for any shop using an auto-discount.

2. **Checkout text is invisible for any visitor on a dark-mode OS or browser.** Every field on the checkout form, and the address-book textarea, renders near-black text on a near-black background due to two shared style files that were missed when the rest of the storefront was fixed for this exact issue. This silently blocks the platform's entire revenue path for a large, unremarkable fraction of visitors, with zero visual indication anything is wrong.

3. **The Nomod payment processor is offered as a fully equal option to Stripe with no warning that it always fails.** A merchant who selects it will have every real checkout attempt 500 at runtime, with nothing in the settings UI hinting this would happen.

4. **Gift-card products have zero admin UI**, despite the feature being fully built and working on the backend and storefront. Merchants cannot enable a real, shipped revenue feature through the product they're paying for.

5. **The Theme Builder hard-crashes when a merchant opens the "Product cards" settings category**, on any theme created before those fields existed — which, given normal theme lifecycle, will be most real merchant themes over time. The fix pattern for this exact bug class already exists in the codebase for one other category but hasn't been applied broadly, meaning other categories are plausibly one click away from the same crash.

6. **Simple-mode shops cannot see an order's delivery date or time anywhere in the Orders UI**, and the documented simple-mode Dashboard was never actually built. For a same-day-delivery flower business specifically, not being able to see when an order is due without switching the whole shop out of simple mode is a fundamental operational gap, not a nice-to-have.

7. **The product wizard's sticky footer silently intercepts clicks on real form controls** — Variants, Attributes, FAQs, Status, and Collections — whenever they scroll into the bottom of the viewport, on both desktop and mobile, with zero visual indication anything is wrong. This quietly breaks core product management for any merchant who happens to scroll to the wrong position.

8. **Decimal values render raw and untrimmed in at least two places** — the Customer Detail order-history total and the Default Delivery Fee setting — showing numbers like `149.00000000000000000000000000000000` instead of `149.00`. Both are one-line fixes using a pattern already applied correctly elsewhere in the same codebase, but as shipped they make the product look badly broken the moment a merchant looks at repeat-customer history or basic delivery settings.

9. **The outlet-address Google Map failed live with a billing/quota error and no graceful fallback**, showing shoppers and merchants a raw, unbranded Google error dialog instead of the app's own designed fallback state. This sits on the exact page used to configure delivery-radius accuracy and is worth resolving at the infrastructure level (checking the Maps API key's billing status) as well as the code level (handling a runtime map failure, not just a script-load failure).

10. **Telr and PayTabs, two of the most common UAE payment gateways, are completely invisible in the admin UI** despite having real backend support and platform-level fallback credentials already built. For a platform explicitly positioned against a UAE-focused competitor, not being able to even see these as options is a concrete, named competitive gap that directly affects whether a UAE merchant can accept payment the way their customers expect to pay.
